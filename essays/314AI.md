##I. Introduction

Begin with an introduction to the role of AI in education and its specific relevance to Software Engineering. Present an overview of the key concepts or tools related to AI that you have used generally and in ICS 314. Mention which AI tools you have made use of e.g. ChatGPT, Bard, Co-Pilot
AI has seen a recent boom in advancement and adoption in many different fields. Its proponents claim that AI is the future and an evolution that will free humans from menial tasks. Detractors say that AI will stunt human growth and take human jobs. From observing my fellow students, and even in my personal life, I feel quite safe in saying that AI is here to stay.
There are various tasks AI can be used for, from tutoring, to medical diagnosis, to writing essays (not this one). AI has evolved into something more than just a glorified search function, now it can anticipate and interpret what you actually need. There are many different models such as Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, and Co-pilot. 
In software engineering, AI is especially helpful when debugging code. Some use it for “vibe-coding”, which is when you describe what you want and the AI model you’re using can determine what you want and create code based on that.

##II. Personal Experience with AI

Prior to this semester I had not used AI in any great capacity. I tried it a bit when it was first getting popular but it was slow and the responses didn’t solve my problem so I abandoned it. Cut to this semester when I tried it again andーwow… what a difference. So much zippier and better at answering correctly.
In ICS 314 I generally tried the exercises on my own then troubleshooted any issues with AI. As the semester progressed I started using it more when I started feeling overwhelmed with the new material I had to learn and remember. Examples:

Experience WODs e.g. E18: For the early Experience WODs I exclusively used my brain and Google to complete them, since I wanted to actually learn the material, but on the later ones I started incorporating AI to provide examples. One prompt was “give an example of a navbar”.
In-class Practice WODs: I did not use AI since it was just for practice and I wanted to learn on my own.
In-class WODs: I generally did not use AI except for some instances when I needed test data or when I tried to fix something but prompting takes time so it generally was not helpful soon enough.
Essays: I prompted AI to provide an outline for an essay once or twice but otherwise the writing was my own.
Final project: I used AI often to quickly understand my teammates’ code and troubleshoot errors. AI was extremely helpful in the project.
Learning a concept / tutorial: I occasionally asked an AI things like “what does detached HEAD mean”.
Answering a question in class or in Discord: Have never used AI for this. It feels very disingenuous.
Asking or answering a smart-question: Somewhat the same as the above category, I assumed people would ask AI their question before posting it.
Coding example e.g. “give an example of using Underscore .pluck”: Yes, I asked AI for examples, e.g. “give an example of a reactjs dropdown menu with a search field”.
Explaining code: I found AI quite helpful in this area as explained in the final project section.
Writing code: Yes, for example in the effort estimation experience I told Claude to make an app that would calculate the time I spent on issues by parsing my reflog data. This did take several rounds to get the result I wanted.
Documenting code: I did not feel the need to do this for ICS 314, except in one case to document all of the database actions I added to my team’s repo. Any other documentation added was without my direction.
Quality assurance e.g. “What’s wrong with this code <code here>” or “Fix the ESLint errors in <code here>”: I often used AI to debug errors. It was helpful to use Claude on my local machine since I could grant access to my files which made it mush more effective at resolving bugs.
Other uses in ICS 314 not listed: None come to mind.

##III. Impact on Learning and Understanding

AI has been immensely helpful this semester, and it definitely helped me to get a handle on complicated topics. That being said I still tried to actually understand what it was helping me with. Current AI still has issues and I believe it is important to view its responses with a critical eye, which means still learning and training your brain enough so that you can recognize problems or articulate them when vibe-coding.

##IV. Practical Applications

There are many applications of  AI outside of coding and chatting. For example, I was part of a project in which we trained an AI agent to win a game using reinforcement learning. AI can also be used in forecasting, warehouse logistics, and autonomous driving. AI is a powerful but developing tool so it needs supervision. As long as we continue to guide AI it can be very useful in software engineering.

##V. Challenges and Opportunities

Since AI is at most in its adolescent stage there are some hiccups. Oftentimes I will have to repeatedly tell it to fix problems that it fixed two prompts ago. It can also be expensive for complex tasks so it is important to prompt effectively and use strategies to limit the thinking the model has to do. In the future I envision a dynamic tutor that will tailor exercises to where the user is at in their understanding and not simply give the answers.

##VI. Comparative Analysis

In traditional teaching students are required to sit and be physically present while the teacher is lecturing, there are textbooks with required reading, and there is homework due the next class time. AI-enhanced approaches could include personalized AI agents for both the students and the teachers, these agents can tailor themselves to the needs and capabilities of their users. In a traditional classroom there are less technological distractions, and in AI-enhanced teaching there could be more distractions. Distractions mean less retention. Traditional teaching methods have a large amount of historic data to back them up, but the content that is being taught necessitates new methods.

##VII. Future Considerations

AI is definitely intertwined with the future of software engineering. There is the danger that companies will try to replace software engineers with AI so it is the job of future software engineers to distinguish themselves in ways that align with AI usage. Humans are still needed for code validation, security checks as a human in the loop, and thinking of the ideas that AI implements.

##VIII. Conclusion

In summation, I am an AI convert. Though it can be used for morally and legally dubious purposes, it is in the end a tool. It is up to us to use it well…  As long as the AI companies remove the extra pointy and poisonous bits. We don’t want any more people developing psychosis or building pipe bombs after all.
